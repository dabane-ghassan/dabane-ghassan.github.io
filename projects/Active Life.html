01

The cell as an agent

Each cell is an agent with a Markov blanket. Its internal state is a belief q(k)q(k) about how many of its eight neighbours are alive. Its sensory states are those eight neighbours. Its active state is its own state ss, which is in turn a sensory state for eight other cells. The plane is tiled by interlocking blankets: every agent is part of its neighbours’ world.

The cell’s generative model is a hidden Markov model over its niche k∈{0,…,8}k \in \{0,\dots,8\}:

p(m1:T,k1:T)=∏tA(mt∣kt) B(kt∣kt−1)p(m_{1:T},k_{1:T}) = \prod_t A(m_t \mid k_t)\, B(k_t \mid k_{t-1})
A(m∣k)=∑jBin(j; k, 1−ε) Bin(m−j; 8−k, ε)A(m\mid k)=\sum_j \mathrm{Bin}(j;\,k,\,1-\varepsilon)\,\mathrm{Bin}(m-j;\,8-k,\,\varepsilon)
B(k∣k′)=1−ρ9+ρ B^(k∣k′)B(k\mid k') = \frac{1-\rho}{9} + \rho\,\hat B(k\mid k')

Here mm is the count the cell perceives through eight channels that each flip with probability ε\varepsilon. The matrix B^\hat B is its world model: either learned from the statistics of noiseless Life, or the belief that the niche does not change. ρ\rho is how much the cell trusts that model.

Perception is Bayesian filtering. The state space is small, so the posterior is exact and variational free energy equals surprise:

qt(k)∝A(mt∣k)∑k′B(k∣k′) qt−1(k′)q_t(k) \propto A(m_t\mid k)\sum_{k'}B(k\mid k')\,q_{t-1}(k')
Ft=−ln⁡∑kA(mt∣k) [B qt−1](k)F_t = -\ln \sum_k A(m_t\mid k)\,[B\,q_{t-1}](k)

The surprise view paints FtF_t; the uncertainty view paints the entropy of qtq_t.

02

Choosing to live

The cell picks its next state aa by minimising expected free energy. Its preferences are over outcomes: the niche it will live in, and whether it stays what it is.

ln⁡p~(a,k∣s)=a C(k)+h [a=s]−ln⁡Z\ln \tilde p(a,k\mid s) = a\,C(k) + h\,[a=s] - \ln Z
G(a)=DKL ⁣[q(o∣a) ∥ p~(o)]⏟risk+Eq H ⁣[A(⋅∣k)]⏟ambiguityG(a) = \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left[q(o\mid a)\,\|\,\tilde p(o)\right]}_{\text{risk}} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}\,\mathrm{H}\!\left[A(\cdot\mid k)\right]}_{\text{ambiguity}}

Sensing is symmetric: each channel carries entropy Hb(ε)\mathrm{H}_b(\varepsilon) whatever its bit, so ambiguity is the same for both actions and risk alone decides. The policy is a softmax over −γG-\gamma G, which for two actions is a logistic:

ln⁡P(a=1)P(a=0)=γ [G(0)−G(1)]=γ(Eqt[C(k)]+h (2s−1))\ln\frac{P(a=1)}{P(a=0)} = \gamma\,\big[G(0)-G(1)\big] = \gamma\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q_t}\big[C(k)\big] + h\,(2s-1)\Big)

γ\gamma is policy precision. C(k)C(k) is a homeostatic preference over crowding. hh is a self-evidencing preference to remain what one is, which acts as a cost of switching state.

03

Conway as a limit

Proposition. As ε→0\varepsilon\to 0 and γ→∞\gamma\to\infty, beliefs collapse onto the true count and the policy becomes deterministic:
st+1=1[C(kt)+h (2st−1)>0]  ⟺  B={k:C(k)>h},    S={k:C(k)>−h}s_{t+1} = \mathbb{1}\big[C(k_t) + h\,(2s_t-1) > 0\big] \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathcal{B} = \{k : C(k) > h\},\;\; \mathcal{S} = \{k : C(k) > -h\}

With C=(−1,−1,0,1,−1,−1,−1,−1,−1)C = (-1,-1,0,1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1) and h=12h = \tfrac12 this is B3/S23. The cell prefers exactly three neighbours, tolerates two and dislikes the rest. The switching cost turns “tolerates two” into survival without birth. Conway’s three rules become one homeostatic preference plus hysteresis.

In simulation the agents reproduce Conway bit for bit: 2,000 steps from random soups on a 96 × 96 torus, for every ρ\rho and both world models.

This decomposition generates exactly the outer-totalistic rules with B⊆S\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{S}, which includes Day & Night, 34 Life, Maze, Coral and Life without Death. Rules such as HighLife (B36/S23) need preferences that depend on the current state, C(s,k)C(s,k).

A Gaussian preference C(k)=θ−(k−μ)2/2σ2C(k) = \theta - (k-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2 also yields B3/S23 (μ=2.75, σ=12, θ=1\mu = 2.75,\ \sigma = \tfrac12,\ \theta = 1), but then

Eq[C]=C(Eqk)−Varq(k)/2σ2\mathbb{E}_q[C] = C(\mathbb{E}_q k) - \mathrm{Var}_q(k)/2\sigma^2

Any uncertainty about the niche is punished quadratically, and noisy populations go extinct almost at once. The bounded preference vector is the better model.

04

What precision does

Away from the limit, the two precisions organise the population into distinct phases.

  • Policy precision. At γ=2\gamma = 2 the population is a hot gas with density 0.39. Density falls as γ\gamma rises, reaches a minimum near γ≈9\gamma \approx 9 (about 0.03), then recovers to Conway’s ash (about 0.05). Mean surprise follows the same curve.
  • Sensory precision. With exact policies, mild noise keeps Life from settling. At ε=0.03\varepsilon = 0.03 the population boils at density 0.27 instead of freezing into ash. At ε=0.04\varepsilon = 0.04 it collapses. Under uncertainty Eq[C]\mathbb{E}_q[C] is pulled toward the average of CC, which is negative, so doubt about the niche reads as a bad niche.
  • World model. A sticky model sustains a dense, slow phase at γ≤4\gamma \le 4 even at ε=0.1\varepsilon = 0.1. The learned model does not; it sustains the boiling ridge instead.

The phase map in the panel shows density over (γ,ε)(\gamma, \varepsilon) for Conway’s preferences. Numbers come from a 96 × 96 torus started at density 0.35, averaged over steps 350 to 500 and two seeds.

05

What a 3 × 3 blanket cannot do

Negative result. Inference does not rescue Life from sensory noise. With the world following Conway and cells sensing it at ε=0.01\varepsilon = 0.01, decisions disagree with Conway’s rule 77 times per 10,000 updates using the raw count, 77 with a flat or learned prior, and 170 with a sticky prior (ρ=0.9\rho = 0.9). A glider breaks after about 14 steps at ε=0.001\varepsilon = 0.001, whatever the prior.

The reason is structural. Information in Life travels one cell per tick, which is exactly the radius of the blanket. A cell’s niche can change completely between two observations, so nothing inside its blanket can tell a real change from a misread neighbour.

Denoising needs a model with a wider reach: cells that infer their neighbours’ niches from a 5 × 5 view, preferences learned from experience, or a hierarchical agent such as a glider treated as a blanket of its own.
